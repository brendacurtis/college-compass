About This Data

Where the numbers come from

Every figure on this site comes from the U.S. Department of Education's College Scorecard, the same federal dataset that powers collegescorecard.ed.gov. It's built from data schools are required to report to the federal government, so it's independent of anything a school's marketing office publishes.

We include the 2,207 four-year, degree-granting colleges and universities currently operating in the dataset — community colleges, certificate-only schools, and closed institutions are left out to keep the search focused on traditional four-year admissions.

How to read the numbers

Net price is what students actually paid on average after grants and scholarships — usually a much more realistic number than the sticker "tuition & fees" price, especially at schools with generous financial aid. The net-price-by-income breakdown on each school's page shows how that number changes for families at different income levels; use it alongside the school's own official Net Price Calculator, linked on every profile, for a personalized estimate.

Diversity figures reflect the current undergraduate student body, self-reported by the institution to the federal government. Special designations like HBCU (Historically Black College or University), HSI (Hispanic-Serving Institution), and PBI (Predominantly Black Institution) are federally defined classifications, not marketing terms.

Outcomes (graduation rates, retention, and post-graduation earnings) are institution-wide averages. They describe the typical student at that school, not any individual student's likely outcome — a strong student can outperform a low average, and vice versa.

Some smaller schools or programs show "Not reported" for certain figures — this usually means the federal government suppresses the number to protect student privacy when a group is very small, not that the school withheld it.

A note on cost and fee waivers

Many families rule schools out based on the sticker price alone. It's worth knowing: most colleges — especially selective private ones — offer need-based aid that can make the real cost far lower than tuition suggests, and application fee waivers are available for families who qualify, no waiting for a counselor required. If cost is a concern, start with the net-price-by-income figures on each school's profile before crossing a school off the list.

Privacy

College Compass doesn't use accounts, doesn't have a server-side database, and doesn't track you. Your application tracker is stored only in your own browser's local storage — it never leaves your device unless you use the "Back Up" button to save your own copy.
